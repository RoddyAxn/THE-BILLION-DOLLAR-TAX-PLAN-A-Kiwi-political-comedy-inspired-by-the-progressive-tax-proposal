THE BILLION-DOLLAR TAX PLAN

A Kiwi political comedy inspired by the progressive-tax proposal shown in the screenshots.

Characters

BILLY — self-appointed Minister of Finance, Economics, Space and Other People’s Money.

MERV — Treasury official who has brought a calculator. This proves to be a mistake.

AUNTY RANGI — taxpayer, realist and owner of the calculator.

SIR REGINALD — billionaire who has suddenly developed an interest in progressive taxation.

NEWSREADER — attempting to explain everything.

SCENE ONE — THE GREAT TAX ANNOUNCEMENT

A government press conference. BILLY walks to the microphone carrying a huge graph.

BILLY:

Ladies and gentlemen, today I am announcing the simplest tax system in the world.

MERV:

How simple?

BILLY:

So simple even Treasury will understand it.

MERV:

Steady on.

BILLY:

Here it is.

First dollar you earn — tax free.

AUNTY RANGI:

Good start.

BILLY:

The billionth dollar you earn — 100 percent tax.

SIR REGINALD:

Terrible finish!

BILLY:

And every dollar after that—

SIR REGINALD:

Don't say it.

BILLY:

Also 100 percent!

SIR REGINALD:

I knew I should've stayed home.

AUNTY RANGI:

Hang on. What happens between one dollar and a billion?

BILLY:

That's the beautiful bit.

He dramatically reveals the graph.

Progressive taxation!

The tax rate rises smoothly as your income rises.

MERV:

What mathematical function are you using?

BILLY:

Function?

Mate, this is government.

We're lucky if the photocopier functions.

SCENE TWO — TREASURY DOES THE MATHS

MERV approaches a whiteboard.

MERV:

We need an equation.

Suppose the marginal tax rate starts at zero and rises linearly until it reaches 100 percent at one billion dollars.

BILLY:

Exactly.

MERV:

Then somebody earning halfway to a billion—

BILLY:

Five hundred million.

MERV:

Correct.

BILLY:

See? I told you Treasury could learn.

MERV:

Their marginal rate near that point would be about 50 percent.

AUNTY RANGI:

But they don't pay 50 percent on everything?

MERV:

No. Only the marginal rate rises progressively.

BILLY:

That's why we draw the graph.

The area underneath the curve is the tax.

AUNTY RANGI:

Ah!

SIR REGINALD:

I don't like this curve.

BILLY:

Why?

SIR REGINALD:

Because I'm underneath it.

SCENE THREE — THE 20 PERCENT PROBLEM

BILLY:

And here's the clever part.

We adjust the curve so government collects approximately 20 percent of GDP.

MERV:

Wait.

BILLY:

What?

MERV:

You can't guarantee that merely by drawing a tax curve.

BILLY:

Why not?

MERV:

Because GDP isn't the same thing as taxable personal income.

Silence.

BILLY:

Merv.

MERV:

Yes?

BILLY:

You've been reading again.

MERV:

There's corporate income, wages, investment income, depreciation, government production—

BILLY:

Stop!

You'll frighten the voters.

AUNTY RANGI:

He's got a point though.

If government wants revenue equal to 20 percent of GDP, wouldn't you have to calibrate the tax curve against the country's actual income distribution?

MERV stares at her.

MERV:

Would you like a job at Treasury?

AUNTY RANGI:

No.

MERV:

Excellent judgment.

SCENE FOUR — THE BILLIONAIRE ARRIVES

SIR REGINALD:

There's another problem.

If everything above one billion dollars is taxed at 100 percent, why would I earn dollar number 1,000,000,001?

BILLY:

National pride.

SIR REGINALD:

No.

BILLY:

Community spirit?

SIR REGINALD:

No.

BILLY:

Free sausage roll?

SIR REGINALD:

What flavour?

MERV:

The economic problem is incentives.

At a 100 percent marginal rate, there's no direct financial reward from additional taxable income.

SIR REGINALD:

Exactly.

I'd stop earning.

AUNTY RANGI:

You personally earn a billion dollars every year?

SIR REGINALD:

Well... technically my companies do.

AUNTY RANGI:

Ah.

BILLY:

Different tax system.

SIR REGINALD:

Trusts.

BILLY:

Different tax system.

SIR REGINALD:

Capital gains.

BILLY:

Different tax system.

SIR REGINALD:

Move overseas.

BILLY:

Different bloody country!

SCENE FIVE — 193 COUNTRIES

A giant United Nations sign descends.

NEWSREADER:

The proposal will now be presented to 193 United Nations member states.

BILLY:

That's the beauty of it.

One equation.

193 countries.

MERV:

Except every country has a different economy.

BILLY:

Minor detail.

MERV:

Different currencies.

BILLY:

Calculator.

MERV:

Different income distributions.

BILLY:

Bigger calculator.

MERV:

Different tax bases, welfare systems, corporate structures and costs of living.

BILLY:

Spreadsheet.

MERV:

Tax avoidance.

BILLY:

Accountant.

MERV:

Tax evasion.

BILLY:

Lawyer.

MERV:

Capital flight.

BILLY:

Airport.

AUNTY RANGI:

He's thought of everything.

SCENE SIX — THE ONE-DOLLAR TAXPAYER

A man enters carrying a one-dollar coin.

MAN:

Excuse me. I earned one dollar this year.

BILLY:

Congratulations.

Tax free.

MAN:

Beauty!

He leaves. Another man enters.

SECOND MAN:

I earned one billion dollars.

Everyone slowly turns toward him.

BILLY:

Congratulations.

SECOND MAN:

Thank you.

BILLY:

Don't move.

SCENE SEVEN — THE REAL QUESTION

AUNTY RANGI:

There's actually an interesting idea hiding underneath all these jokes.

BILLY:

Don't ruin the show.

AUNTY RANGI:

Instead of arguing endlessly about dozens of tax brackets, you define a mathematical curve.

Low incomes face very low marginal rates.

As income rises, the marginal rate rises.

Then you calibrate the curve to collect whatever total revenue society decides government needs.

MERV:

That's the serious version.

AUNTY RANGI:

But the billion-dollar ceiling isn't automatically the right number.

MERV:

Correct.

AUNTY RANGI:

And 20 percent of GDP doesn't automatically fall out of the equation.

MERV:

Correct.

AUNTY RANGI:

And we'd still need rules for companies, trusts, investment income and avoidance.

MERV:

Correct.

BILLY:

Well, you've completely stuffed up my three-word election slogan.

FINALE

NEWSREADER:

So what has the Government decided?

BILLY:

After extensive consultation, economic modelling and approximately seven minutes on the calculator—

Drum roll.

—we have discovered the fundamental principle of taxation.

NEWSREADER:

Which is?

BILLY:

If you've got one dollar...

we don't want it.

If you've got a billion dollars...

SIR REGINALD:

Yes?

BILLY:

We'd like a meeting.

SIR REGINALD:

And if I've got two billion?

BILLY:

We'll send the whole Cabinet.

AUNTY RANGI:

What if I've got nothing?

BILLY:

Then congratulations.

You've already completed your tax return.

Blackout.

END
